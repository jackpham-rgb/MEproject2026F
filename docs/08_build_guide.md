# Build Guide: Concept A

Everything needed to go from files to a running robot, in order. Generated by `backend/tools/make_build_guide.py` from the BOM and the print files.

**Order of work:** buy → print the coupon → adjust fits → print the rest → set the buck → assemble with the servo-setup sketch → wire → calibrate → test.

## 2. Printing

Files: `cad/print/stl/` (already turned to the best side and placed on the bed). Orientation preview: `cad/print/print_orientations.png`. Bed size assumed 220 × 220 mm.

**PETG settings (start here, tune for your printer):** nozzle 0.4 mm at 235 °C, bed 80 °C, layer 0.2 mm, 3 walls (1.2 mm), 4 top / 4 bottom layers, 30 % gyroid infill, cooling fan 30–50 %, elephant-foot compensation 0.15 mm (holes on the bed side), print speed ~60 mm/s. Use glue stick on smooth PEI so PETG doesn't weld to the bed.

## 3. Before any servo is connected: set the buck

1. Connect only the 12 V adapter → DC jack → XL4015 IN+ / IN−. Nothing on the output.
2. Meter on OUT+ / OUT−. Turn the pot (it usually needs many turns) until it reads **5.8 V**.
3. Unplug. Mark the pot with a dot of nail polish so it isn't turned by accident.

## 4. Assembly

Servos are fitted **at their HOME angle**, so the horns go on at the right orientation. Load `firmware/servo_setup/servo_setup.ino` on the Uno, wire the servos as in section 5, power up: every servo goes to HOME (turntable 135°, lift 30°, wrist 0°, gripper 20°). Type `t 135`, `l 30`, etc. in the Serial Monitor (9600 baud, Newline) to move one.

Fasteners: #6-32 × ½″ ×8 (tray 2, spine 2, top cap 2, link pivot 1, right gear axle 1); #8-32 × ½″ ×4 (MG996R); servo-bag screws for horns and servo tabs.

### 4.1 Base (CA-101)

1. Press the 6807 bearing into the tower pocket (Ø47.1). Press on the **outer** race only, evenly, with a flat block in a vise. It should sit flat on the pocket floor.
2. Fit the MG996R in its pocket under the tower, spline up through the bearing bore, body pointing away from the arm sweep. 4 × #8-32 × ½″ up through the flange into the printed pilots.
3. Fill the two corner pockets with steel washers (~100 g total). Stick 4 rubber feet under the base.

### 4.2 Turret (CA-201)

1. Servo at **135°** (`t 135`). Put the round horn on the spline so its screw holes sit at 45°, 135°, 225°, 315°, and fit its centre screw.
2. Lower the turret plate: the hub presses into the bearing bore (Ø34.95), and the plate's front (the rod bosses) points at the START can.
3. 4 servo-bag screws from above through the plate into the horn.
4. `t 45` then `t 135`: the plate turns 90° freely, with no rubbing.

### 4.3 Column and lift (CA-202, CA-203, CA-301, CA-302)

1. Press 2 LM6UU into each rod bore of the carriage (CA-301), flush with the faces.
2. Press the two 112 mm rods into the turret plate's blind holes (Ø6.0).
3. Slide the carriage down over both rods. It must drop under its own weight; if it sticks, the rods aren't parallel yet (the top cap fixes that).
4. Spine (CA-202) on the turret plate: 2 × #6-32 × ½″ from above. Top cap (CA-203) over the rod tops and onto the spine: 2 × #6-32 × ½″. Check again that the carriage slides freely.
5. Lift servo (MG90S) into the spine bracket, 2 servo-bag screws. Servo at **30°** (`l 30`).
6. Crank: an MG90S single-arm horn trimmed to a 20 mm pin radius, fitted at 30° so the carriage is at the bottom of its travel. Lift link (CA-302): crank end with a servo-bag screw, carriage end with #6-32 × ½″ as the pivot (don't over-tighten: it must turn).
7. `l 75` then `l 30`: the carriage rises 15 mm and returns, smoothly.

### 4.4 Wrist and gripper (CA-401, CA-402, CA-403, CA-404)

1. Wrist servo (MG90S) into the carriage's wrist bracket, 2 servo-bag screws. Servo at **0°** (`w 0`). Round horn on, centre screw in.
2. Gripper frame (CA-401) flange onto the horn, gripper **upright** (plates horizontal, can opening facing out): 4 servo-bag screws.
3. Gripper servo (SG90) into the frame, 2 servo-bag screws. Servo at **20°** (`g 20`).
4. Right finger (CA-402) on its axle: #6-32 × ½″ from the top, through the 3.9 mm bore, into the bottom plate's pilot. It must swing freely.
5. Left finger (CA-403) onto the SG90 spline (its socket replaces the horn), gears meshed so both arms are open by the same angle. Horn screw in.
6. `g 95`: both arms close together and the pads meet the can position evenly. `g 20` opens.
7. `w 180` then `w 0`: the gripper rolls a full half-turn. **If the wrist stops short of 180°, note the angle: this is the risk to settle first** (try another MG90S).

### 4.5 Electronics tray (CA-102)

1. Uno and XL4015 onto their printed pegs, a dab of hot glue on each. Mini breadboard: self-adhesive.
2. START button (12 mm), LED (5 mm) and DC jack (8 mm) through the front panel.
3. Tray onto the base: 2 × #6-32 × ½″ up through the tabs.

## 5. Wiring

Follow `docs/04_wiring.md` and the diagram `docs/wiring/wiring_diagram.png`:

1. Jack + → XL4015 IN+ and Uno VIN. Jack − → XL4015 IN− and Uno GND.
2. XL4015 OUT+ / OUT− → breadboard rail + / −. 1000 µF capacitor across the rail (− leg to −).
3. **MG996R red / brown straight into the XL4015 OUT terminals** (cut one extension lead to split them from the orange signal wire). Signal → D9.
4. Lift, wrist, gripper through 300 mm extensions to the rail; signals → D10, D6, D5.
5. Rail − → Uno GND (common ground).
6. Button: D4 → button → GND. LED: D13 → 220 Ω → LED long leg; short leg → GND.
7. Route the column servo leads down the spine with a loose loop: the turret turns 90° and the carriage moves 15 mm. Tie the loop to the spine so it can't catch the arm.

## 6. Calibration (servo_setup sketch)

Place a real empty can at the START mark (5.0 in from the turntable axis), against the can bumper.

| Constant | How to find it | Default |
|---|---|---:|
| `TURN_START` | `t` until the gripper is centred on the START can | 135 |
| `TURN_TARGET` | `t` until the gripper is centred on the TARGET mark (90° away) | 45 |
| `LIFT_DOWN` | `l` until the pads are at the can's mid-height and the can just touches the table | 30 |
| `LIFT_UP` | `LIFT_DOWN` + 45 (0.6 in higher); check the flipped can clears the table | 75 |
| `WRIST_FLIP` | `w` until the gripper is exactly upside down (use a phone level app on the frame) | 180 |
| `GRIP_CLOSED` | `g` until the pads touch the can, then +3 to +5° for a light squeeze (0.5–1 N per arm: the can shouldn't dent, and shouldn't slide when you tug it) | 95 |
| `GRIP_RELEASE` | upside down at the target: open until the pads just clear the can (≥ 2 mm) | 70 |

`p` prints all four angles. Copy them into the constants at the top of `firmware/concept_a/concept_a.ino`, upload it, then press START.

## 7. Tests (fill in the report's section 10)

| Test | Pass |
|---|---|
| Buck output under a full run (meter on the rail) | ≥ 5.5 V |
| Wrist range | 180° ± 3° |
| Turntable | 90° ± 2°, can within 0.17 in of the target |
| Grip | holds through the run, no dent, SG90 not hot |
| Full run × 10 | can inverted and free-standing at the target in ≥ 9 of 10 |

If a run fails, the Serial Monitor shows the last step reached (`> GRIP`, `> LIFT`, …). Fix that step's angle and run again.
